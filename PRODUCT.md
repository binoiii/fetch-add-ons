# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Pet owners who want to feel good about their pet's health coverage. In the context of this project, the primary audience is the Fetch Pet Health hiring team — engineers and designers evaluating frontend craft, interaction quality, and product intuition. They open the URL and form an impression within seconds.

## Product Purpose

A pet care plan builder where users select their pet (dog or cat), then toggle care add-ons. As add-ons are selected, the pet visually evolves — happier expression, shinier coat, glowing aura. A live health score climbs in real time. The final screen celebrates the completed plan.

The product succeeds when it makes pet care feel warm, personal, and rewarding rather than transactional.

## Brand Personality

Warm, confident, quietly playful. The emotional register of a premium wellness brand, not a form. Think: "your pet deserves this."

## Anti-references

- Insurance software and enterprise dashboards (cold grids, data-heavy layouts, form-first thinking)
- Clinical / veterinary aesthetics (cold blues, sterile whites, medical-form structure)
- Cute or kiddy aesthetics (cartoon fonts, bubble UI, childish illustrations, excessive emojis)
- Purple-gradient SaaS defaults
- Generic card grids with icon + heading + text repeated endlessly

## Design Principles

1. **Feel before function.** The emotional response comes first. Every interaction should make the user feel something before they read anything.
2. **Motion earns meaning.** Animations exist to reinforce state changes, not to decorate. The pet's evolution IS the feedback.
3. **Restraint over richness.** When in doubt, remove. A well-placed detail lands harder than a busy screen.
4. **Premium through precision.** Spacing, weight contrast, and timing communicate quality. Rounded corners and pink alone do not.
5. **Product intuition over portfolio tricks.** Decisions should be explainable as product choices, not aesthetic preferences.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best effort. Semantic HTML, keyboard navigability, and visible focus states. No hard WCAG compliance target.
